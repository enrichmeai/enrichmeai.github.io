Reading list

Books that shaped how this guide thinks about health, character, money and ideas. Not technical manuals: the engineering bookshelf is on Why this guide exists. Read slowly, take what helps, and leave the rest.

Health and habits

  • The 4 Pillar Plan, Dr Rangan Chatterjee (2017). A doctor's plan for health built on four everyday pillars, small changes made consistently.
  • The 5 AM Club, Robin Sharma (2018). Owning the start of your day before the world asks for it.
  • The Everyday Hero Manifesto, Robin Sharma (2021). Doing ordinary work, every day, to a high standard.
  • Atomic Habits, James Clear (2018). Change by small steps: build a habit by making it small and easy, and aim to get about 1% better each day.
  • Psycho-Cybernetics, Maxwell Maltz (1960). How your self-image sets the limits of what you attempt, and how to change it.

See Body, Mind & Nature.

Mind and character

  • As a Man Thinketh, James Allen (1903). A short book on the power of thought: by working patiently on our thoughts we can remake our lives and change our circumstances. Old enough to be free to read: it is on Wikisource.
  • Meditations, Marcus Aurelius. Private notes on Stoic philosophy, written by a Roman emperor to himself, much of it while on military campaign. Nearly two thousand years old and still practical: control what you can, accept what you cannot, do your duty well.

See Philosophy and Being a Good Human.

Money, work and building

  • The Almanack of Naval Ravikant, compiled by Eric Jorgenson (2020). Naval Ravikant's thinking on wealth, judgement and happiness, gathered from his talks and writing.
  • Zero to One, Peter Thiel with Blake Masters (2014). Building something new rather than copying what exists; it grew out of Thiel's startup class at Stanford.
  • I Will Teach You to Be Rich, Ramit Sethi (2009, updated 2019). A beginner's guide to managing money, written for people starting out.
  • The Psychology of Money, Morgan Housel (2020). Why behaviour matters more than intelligence when it comes to money.

Thinking and science

  • The Beginning of Infinity: Explanations that Transform the World, David Deutsch (2011). Progress comes from good explanations, and there is no limit to how far it can go.

See Science & Method.

Why a reading list on an engineering site

Technology changes every year; these ideas have lasted far longer. Looking after your health, mastering your own thinking, being careful with money and valuing good explanations make a better engineer and a better person. If AI saves you an hour, spend some of it on a chapter of one of these. Suggest a book through the ask-on-GitHub link at the end of any page.
