Everyday science: things we should all know

Basic science for everyone, not only engineers: the reasons behind things we see every day. A grounded person knows why the sky is blue as well as how to use a phone. See Science & Method for how we know.

Sky, light and weather

  • Why the sky is blue: air scatters blue light much more than red or green, so blue reaches our eyes from every direction of the sky.
  • Rainbows: sunlight is bent (refracted), reflected inside water droplets and split into its colours (dispersion).
  • Lightning and thunder: light arrives almost at once; sound travels at about 343 metres a second in air at 20 °C. Count the seconds between flash and thunder and divide by three for kilometres (by five for miles).
  • The speed of light is exactly 299,792,458 metres a second, the fastest anything can travel.

Earth, Moon and seasons

  • Seasons come from the tilt of Earth's axis, not its distance from the Sun: as Earth orbits, each hemisphere leans towards the Sun for part of the year.
  • Tides are the rise and fall of the sea caused mainly by the gravity of the Moon and the Sun.
  • Moon phases: we see different parts of the Moon's sunlit half as it orbits us, from crescent to full and back.

Water and heat

  • Ice floats because water expands by about 9% when it freezes, so ice is less dense than liquid water. Lakes freeze from the top, which lets life survive underneath.
  • Boiling point drops with pressure: under lower pressure, such as high in the mountains, water boils at a lower temperature, so food takes longer to cook.
  • The greenhouse effect: heat-trapping gases in the atmosphere stop Earth losing heat to space, raising its surface temperature. Some keeps the planet habitable; adding more warms it.

Life

  • Photosynthesis: plants, algae and some bacteria turn the energy in sunlight into chemical energy, the base of almost every food chain.
  • Your body changes with use: muscles, bones and the brain all respond to how you use them. See Body, Mind & Nature.
