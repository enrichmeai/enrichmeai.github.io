Guide / User interfaces / Design Thinking & UX

Design thinking and user experience

Building the right thing before building it right: understand the people, frame the problem, try ideas cheaply, and test with real users. It applies to an API, an internal tool or a data product as much as to an app.

Design thinking

  • Start with empathy: understand the people you are designing for, their goals, context and frustrations, before you decide what to build.
  • Frame the problem in their words. A well-framed problem is half solved; a badly framed one gets a polished answer to the wrong question.
  • Generate many ideas, then sketch and prototype the promising ones as cheaply as possible.
  • Test and iterate: put the prototype in front of real people, learn, and change it. Repeat.

The Double Diamond

A design process model popularised by the British Design Council in 2005, in four phases:

  1. Discover: go wide; research the problem and the people.
  2. Define: narrow down to the real problem.
  3. Develop: go wide again; explore possible solutions.
  4. Deliver: narrow down; test, choose and ship.

Two diamonds: diverge then converge on the problem, then on the solution.

UX practices engineers can use

  • Talk to users before building, and watch them use what you built. What people do often differs from what they say.
  • Usability testing with a few people finds most big problems: Jakob Nielsen's advice is to test with no more than five users and run as many small tests as you can afford.
  • Heuristic evaluation: review a design against a short list of usability principles, a method developed by Rolf Molich and Jakob Nielsen.
  • Personas and journeys keep real people in mind: who they are, what they are trying to do, and where it goes wrong.

Good UX is good engineering

  • Clear errors: say what went wrong and how to fix it, in plain words. See APIs for good error responses.
  • Fast feels good: speed is part of the experience. See Performance and Core Web Vitals on Web Fundamentals.
  • Consistency: the same thing looks and behaves the same everywhere.
  • Accessible by default: design for everyone from the start. See Accessibility.
