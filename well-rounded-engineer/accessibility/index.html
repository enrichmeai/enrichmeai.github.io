Guide / User interfaces / Accessibility

Accessibility: software for everyone

People use software with screen readers, keyboards, voice, magnification and captions, permanently or for a while after an injury. Accessible design helps all of them, and usually makes the product better for everyone else too.

WCAG 2.2 in one card

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are the W3C standard for accessible web content; WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation on 5 October 2023. They are organised under four principles, often remembered as POUR:

PrincipleMeansExamples
PerceivablePeople can see or hear the content, one way or anotherText alternatives for images, captions for video, enough contrast
OperablePeople can use it, whatever their inputEverything works by keyboard, no keyboard traps, targets big enough to hit
UnderstandablePeople can follow itThe page language is declared, labels and errors are clear, behaviour is predictable
RobustIt works with assistive technologyEvery control exposes a name, role and value

Each success criterion has a level, A, AA or AAA; AA is the common target. Two AA examples: normal text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, and in 2.2 pointer targets should be at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels, with some exceptions.

Start with semantic HTML

<!-- a real button: focusable, works with Enter and Space, announced as a button -->
<button type="button">Save order</button>

<!-- every input has a label -->
<label for="email">Email</label>
<input id="email" type="email" autocomplete="email">

<!-- meaningful image: describe it; decorative image: empty alt -->
<img src="chart.png" alt="Orders rose 20% in March">
<img src="divider.png" alt="">

Native HTML elements come with keyboard support and the right role for free. Reach for ARIA only when no native element does the job.

Test it

  • Unplug the mouse: can you reach and use everything with Tab, Enter, Space and the arrow keys, and always see where the focus is?
  • Use a screen reader on your own pages for ten minutes; it changes how you write markup.
  • Zoom to 200% and use a phone-width screen: does anything break or hide?
  • Automated checks (axe, Lighthouse) catch a share of issues; they cannot judge whether alt text or labels make sense. Do both. See Testing.

It is also the law

  • European Union: under the European Accessibility Act, as of 28 June 2025, newly marketed products and services it covers must be accessible.
  • Many countries have similar laws for public-sector and commercial services. Check the rules where your users are.
  • Beyond the law: captions help people in noisy places, contrast helps in sunlight, keyboard support helps power users. Accessibility is quality.

Checked against: WCAG 2.2 (W3C); Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (Wikipedia); WAI-ARIA (Wikipedia); European Accessibility Act (Wikipedia); MDN: Accessibility.
